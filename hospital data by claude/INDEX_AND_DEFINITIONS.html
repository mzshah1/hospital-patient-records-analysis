📖 Section 1: Key Definitions & Terminology

Purpose: These definitions ensure consistent understanding of terms used throughout the analysis and healthcare context.

Core Healthcare Terms

Patient
An individual receiving healthcare services. Identified by a unique patient ID in the database. Includes demographic information (age, gender, race, ethnicity, location, contact details).
Encounter
A single healthcare visit or interaction between a patient and the healthcare system. Can be in-person (clinic, hospital, ER) or virtual. Each encounter has a start time, stop time, associated costs, and encounter classification. Examples: office visit, emergency room visit, hospital admission.
Encounter Class
The category or type of encounter. In this dataset: Ambulatory, Outpatient, Emergency, Inpatient, Urgent Care, Wellness. Determines the care setting and typically correlates with cost.
Procedure
A medical or surgical intervention performed during an encounter. Includes tests, surgeries, injections, diagnostic procedures, etc. Each procedure has a cost and may have an associated reason code (diagnosis).
Payer
An insurance company or entity responsible for covering healthcare costs. Examples: Medicare, Medicaid, Blue Cross, Aetna. The payer determines coverage limits and reimbursement rates. In this dataset: Medicare (63%), Medicaid (27%), Commercial (10%).
Organization
A healthcare facility (hospital, clinic, urgent care center) where patient encounters occur. In this dataset: Massachusetts General Hospital (single organization).

Financial Terms

Base Encounter Cost
The fixed cost of the encounter itself, excluding line items for medications, procedures, and other services. Represents facility/provider time.
Total Claim Cost
The complete cost of an encounter including all services: base cost + procedures + medications + supplies. This is what gets billed to the payer.
Payer Coverage
The amount of the total claim cost that the insurance payer covers. The difference between total cost and payer coverage is patient responsibility (copay/coinsurance).

Clinical & Coding Terms

Reason Code (Diagnosis Code)
A SNOMED-CT code identifying the medical condition or diagnosis that prompted the encounter or procedure. 77.5% of procedures in this dataset lack reason codes - a significant data quality issue.
SNOMED-CT Code
Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine Clinical Terms. A standardized medical coding system used for diagnoses, procedures, and clinical observations. Ensures consistency in medical records.
Comorbidity
The presence of one or more diseases/conditions co-occurring in the same patient. In this analysis: 47.7% of patients with emergency encounters suggests complex care needs/comorbidities.

Analysis-Specific Terms

Risk Quintile (Q1-Q5)
Patients divided into 5 equal groups based on total healthcare costs. Q1 = lowest 20% of costs; Q5 = highest 20% of costs. Q5 patients: 70% mortality, $621K avg cost.
Mortality Rate
Percentage of patients who died during the study period. Overall: 15.8% (154/974 patients). By age group: 27.5% in 80+ age group - indicates mortality risk increases significantly with age.
Utilization
The rate at which patients use healthcare services. Measured as encounters per patient, procedures per encounter, or emergency visit frequency. High utilization in specific patient subgroups drives costs.

🏥 Section 2: Encounter Types in Healthcare Context

Key Insight: Different encounter types serve different purposes, have different costs, and require different care settings. Understanding each is critical for healthcare operations.

Encounter Type Breakdown (27,891 Total)

Encounter Type Count % of Total Avg Cost Definition & Context
Ambulatory 12,537 45% Variable Scheduled outpatient visits to physician offices or clinics. Routine, planned care. Lowest complexity. Examples: annual checkup, medication refill visit.
Outpatient 6,300 23% $2,237 Hospital-based visits without admission. Same-day care (patient goes home). Moderate complexity. Examples: lab work, imaging, minor procedures.
Urgent Care 3,666 13% Variable Non-emergency but time-sensitive visits. Less urgent than ER, more urgent than routine. Examples: sprained ankle, minor cuts, respiratory infection.
Emergency 2,322 8% High Unplanned, acute care visits. Highest complexity and highest cost per visit. Examples: chest pain, serious injury, acute illness. 465 patients (47.7%) have ER visits - preventability opportunity.
Inpatient 1,135 4% $7,761 Highest cost encounter type. Hospital admission requiring overnight stay. Most complex cases. Examples: surgery, major illness management, ICU care. Length-of-stay optimization critical.
Wellness 1,931 7% Variable Preventive care and health maintenance visits. Routine checkups, screenings, vaccinations. Lowest cost but most valuable for prevention.

Clinical Implications by Encounter Type

⚠️ Emergency Utilization Crisis: 2,322 emergency encounters (8% of total) from 465 patients (47.7%) indicates major primary care access gaps. These are often preventable with better outpatient management.
✓ Inpatient Opportunity: Although only 4% of encounters, inpatient visits are 3.5x more expensive than outpatient. Reducing length-of-stay by 1 day = significant cost savings.
📊 Strategic Focus: The mix of encounter types reveals care delivery patterns. High emergency % (8%) vs. wellness (7%) suggests reactive rather than preventive care model.

Year-over-Year Analysis:

  • Years with increasing costs = either more procedures or higher costs per encounter
  • Years with increasing unique patients = larger patient population served
  • Recent stability = consistent recent utilization pattern

Quarterly Trends

Examination of data by quarter (Q1-Q4) reveals seasonal patterns in healthcare utilization.

Quarterly Insight: 45 quarters analyzed across the dataset. Compare Q1 vs Q2 vs Q3 vs Q4 to identify seasonal variations:
  • Q1 (Jan-Mar): Typically higher utilization post-holiday
  • Q2 (Apr-Jun): Variable, often lower utilization
  • Q3 (Jul-Sep): Summer typically lower
  • Q4 (Oct-Dec): Higher due to holiday season and year-end healthcare usage

How to Use Temporal Data

  • Budget Planning: Use yearly/quarterly trends to forecast future costs
  • Staffing: Adjust staffing based on seasonal utilization patterns
  • Resource Allocation: Surge planning for high-utilization months
  • Performance Tracking: Compare current month/quarter to same period previous year (YoY comparison)
  • Trend Detection: Identify if recent trends are improving or worsening

📋 Section 4: Complete Data Dictionary

This comprehensive data dictionary explains every field in the dataset. Organized by table.

PATIENTS Table

Field Name Data Type Description
id Integer (PK) Unique patient identifier
birth_date Date (YYYY-MM-DD) Patient's date of birth
death_date Date (NULL if living) Patient's date of death, if applicable
gender Text (M/F) Patient's gender (M=Male, F=Female)
race Text Patient's primary race (White, Black, Asian, etc.)
ethnicity Text Patient's primary ethnicity
address, city, state, zip Text Patient's residential location

ENCOUNTERS Table

Field Name Data Type Description
id Integer (PK) Unique encounter identifier
patient_id Integer (FK) Foreign key to PATIENTS table
start_time DateTime (ISO8601 UTC) When the encounter started (yyyy-MM-dd'T'HH:mm'Z')
stop_time DateTime (ISO8601 UTC) When the encounter ended
encounter_class Text Type of encounter: ambulatory, outpatient, emergency, inpatient, urgent care, wellness
base_encounter_cost Decimal Fixed cost of encounter (excluding procedures/medications)
total_claim_cost Decimal Total cost including all line items
payer_coverage Decimal Amount covered by insurance payer
reason_code SNOMED-CT Code Diagnosis code (PRIMARY DIAGNOSIS for encounter)

PROCEDURES Table

Field Name Data Type Description
id Integer (PK) Auto-generated unique procedure identifier
patient_id Integer (FK) Foreign key to PATIENTS table
encounter_id Integer (FK) Foreign key to ENCOUNTERS table (which encounter was this procedure performed in)
start_time DateTime When the procedure was performed
code SNOMED-CT Code Standardized procedure code
description Text Human-readable description of procedure
base_cost Decimal Cost of this specific procedure
reason_code SNOMED-CT Code (77.5% NULL) ⚠️ MAJOR DATA QUALITY ISSUE: Why was this procedure performed? Missing in 36,945/47,701 records.

PAYERS & ORGANIZATIONS Tables

Table Primary Keys Contains
PAYERS id (Insurance Company ID) Insurance payers (Medicare, Medicaid, Blue Cross, etc.) - name, HQ location, phone
ORGANIZATIONS id (Facility ID) Healthcare facilities - in this dataset: only Massachusetts General Hospital
